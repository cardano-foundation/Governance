## Summary

The meeting focused on a detailed review of nine IO concurrent governance actions, identifying workstream overlaps and the excessive allocation of personnel. A primary concern was raised regarding a "maintenance" proposal that appears to aggregate existing research efforts, potentially obscuring actual financial requirements. The team finalized a strategic voting position for the revised Cardano Summit proposal to ensure a community-led outcome. Additionally, reservations were expressed regarding the current level of administrative oversight for funded milestones in 2025, emphasizing a need for more rigorous verification by budget administrators.

## Details

- **Review of IO Proposals:** An initial review of newly submitted governance actions identified extensive overlaps and the potential re-bundling of work across different technical submissions. Specifically, a "maintenance proposal" appeared to aggregate workstreams and personnel already covered by existing long-term research efforts spanning 18 months. Analysis suggests that multiple FTEs may have been double-counted across various proposals. The team concluded that while individual workstreams may have merit, the maintenance proposal—as currently structured—lacks sufficient detail to justify its separate funding request and may be recommended for rejection to ensure fiscal responsibility. Further clarity will be sought from the proposer where possible.
- **Analysis of Project Funding:** Technical scrutiny of specific initiatives, such as the Leois project, revealed significant discrepancies in resource allocation. While the project was originally intended to be funded for two years under a previous research effort with eight FTEs, current proposals appear to allocate up to 25 FTEs to a project team. This redundancy highlights a broader issue in current submission tactics where technical work is fragmented across multiple proposals to increase overall funding draws. The team affirmed that rejecting redundant maintenance proposals while potentially accepting core workstreams is the most pragmatic path to preserving treasury integrity.
- **Governance Proposal Overload and Voter Engagement:** The ecosystem is currently experiencing a record volume of concurrent governance actions, with ten submissions occurring within a single week. This "proposal overload" has led to observed voter fatigue and lower turnout among DReps. Internal discussion addressed the complexity of this environment, noting that voting against major institutional proposals carries potential risks for future partnership opportunities. To mitigate these risks and maintain technical independence, the team emphasized the necessity of providing clear, evidence-based rationales for every vote, ensuring that decisions are perceived as objective technical assessments.
- **Strategic Voting Position for Revised Summit Proposals:** Regarding the Cardano Summit, the team addressed the existence of a revised proposal that requests a modified amount of ADA. To prevent the perception that the Foundation is exerting undue control over governance outcomes, a consensus was reached to record an early "abstain" vote for both the original and revised summit actions. The supporting rationale will clearly state that the abstention is due to the proposals being a resubmission and to allow for a purely community-led mandate. This strategy is intended to signal neutrality while ensuring the Foundation remains a participating observer in the decision-making process without tipping the scales of the majority sentiment.
- **Administrative Oversight and Milestone Verification Accountability:** Significant reservations were expressed regarding the current reporting standards for past technical projects. It was noted that certain entities claimed near-total delivery of work (approx. 89%) while only requesting 48% of the corresponding milestone-based funds, suggesting a disconnect between progress reporting and financial accountability. The team raised concerns on potentially passive existing budget administrators, who appear to be monitoring fund flows without performing rigorous technical verification of milestone deliveries. There is a strong recommendation for a more active administrative model where payouts are strictly conditioned on verified technical evidence.
