## Summary

The meeting addressed significant irregularities in the recent Intersect Committee elections, which resulted in a substantial reduction of the valid vote count. The team reached a consensus to support the TOKEN2049 sponsorship upgrades while rejecting the Gerolamo browser-node proposal due to technical feasibility concerns. Conversely, the Pebble governance action was recommended for approval to improve developer choice. A major technical analysis of current IOG proposals was reviewed, leading to a strategic decision to request additional transparency and accountability regarding high personnel costs and milestone reporting before casting votes.

## Details

- **Intersect Committee Election Irregularities and Audit Results:** The meeting opened with a report on significant issues identified during the Intersect Committee elections, impacting voting results and leading to disqualifying candidates. It was observed that valid voting participation has dropped significantly compared to previous elections. This trend suggests a growing sense of fatigue among the voting community.
- **Cardano at Token 2049 Sponsorship Strategy:** The team evaluated two resubmitted proposals for the TOKEN2049 event: a baseline platinum sponsorship (approx. 3.3 million ADA) and a supplemental title sponsorship top-up. Review confirmed that the title sponsorship is necessary to secure a primary keynote slot and a higher level of global media reach that was inaccessible during the previous year. While the cost has increased due to market volatility, the team reached a consensus to recommend a "yes" vote for both proposals to maintain consistency with the Foundation's long-term marketing strategy and to ensure a competitive institutional presence in the Asian market.
- **Technical Feasibility of Gerolamo Proposal:** The resubmitted Gerolamo browser-node proposal was rejected following a technical assessment of its feasibility. Primary concerns involve the inherent difficulty of storing the blockchain ledger state within a browser's index DB, especially considering future network scaling requirements. Furthermore, it was noted that advanced alternatives for browser-integrated nodes already exist, rendering Gerolamo's unique selling point redundant. The advisory team concluded that the treasury should prioritize infrastructure that meaningfully drives adoption, and that the Gerolamo project may be better suited for grassroots or community-level funding rather than a large-scale technical grant.
- **Pebble Funding and Smart Contract Language Diversity:** In contrast to the Gerolamo assessment, the team recommended a "yes" vote for the Pebble programming language. While the requested funding for production readiness and TypeScript tooling maintenance remains high, the technical value of providing developers with more language choice and familiar syntax was affirmed. The discussion emphasized that the complexity of a smart contract platform, including its compiler, language server, and editor integrations, justifies a dedicated engineering budget. The Foundation views Pebble as a strategic investment to lower the barrier to entry for developers comfortable with web-standard technologies.
- **Analysis of Contributor Activity of IO TWGAs:** A detailed technical audit of contributor activity revealed that current institutional proposals carry an estimated cost of approximately $400,000 per engineer. Significant concern was expressed regarding the lack of transparency in several submissions, specifically a proposal requesting $600,000 for the drafting of a single CIP.
