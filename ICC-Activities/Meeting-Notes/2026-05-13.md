## Summary

The meeting centered on a comprehensive technical review of four major treasury withdrawal proposals submitted by IO, focusing heavily on budget transparency, cost-effectiveness, and strategic leverage. The team expressed concerns regarding the Developer Experience Initiative due to its short-term nature and lack of strategic planning. While the Cardano Upgrades proposal was generally supported, the Consensus Initiatives governance action raised concerns over budget opacity and overall readiness. Finally, the Cardano Maintenance Initiative was met with strong opposition due to historical complexities, high costs, and potential conflicts of interest within its proposed technical oversight structure.

## Details

- **Developer Experience Proposal:** The team reviewed the 3.6 million ada request aimed at streamlining developer documentation, creating a unified CLI tool, and establishing a developer hub. Subject matter experts raised concerns regarding the cost-effectiveness of this 6-month proposal, noting that the development team is not yet established and would likely be hired externally post-funding. Due to the lack of concrete work plans for the contract libraries and the absence of a long-term transition strategy, the consensus recommended against funding at this time, asserting that the ecosystem's immediate bottlenecks are liquidity and user volume rather than developer tooling.
- **Cardano Upgrades Proposal:** The technical review of the 13 million ada upgrades proposal focused on three core objectives: implementing account address enhancements to remove deposit restrictions, defining multi-asset treasury specifications, and implementing native Babel fees. While the account address enhancements and multi-asset treasury goals were strongly supported as critical safeguards against volatility, the native Babel fees component was met with skepticism due to the extremely low utilization of existing non-native implementations. Additionally, reviewers highlighted significant budget opacity, noting that 86% of the $3 million USD budget is allocated generally to engineering with minimal transparency regarding ecosystem integration and support.
- **Consensus Initiative:** The team evaluated the Consensus Initiatives proposal, which requests 27 million ada to finalize the implementation of Leios following a prior research expenditure of over $8 million USD. While the implementation is recognized as vital for the network's long-term competitiveness, concerns were raised regarding the high, unspecified budget and the lack of clarity on hard-fork readiness criteria. The strategic discussion emphasized that although the ecosystem is highly dependent on the vendor's technical delivery, it would be wise to request structural reform of the proposal to enhance its transparency.
- **Cardano Maintenance Proposal:** A critical evaluation of the Cardano Maintenance proposal highlighted significant technical and structural complexities. The proposal mirrors workstreams designed a year ago by the former Intersect technical steering committee and involves a partnership between the primary developer and an external firm established by previously departed technical leads. Reservations were expressed regarding the proposed governance structure, where technical oversight and milestone verification would be managed by committees containing individuals directly involved in the proposal's delivery, creating a conflict of interest. The bundled nature of the proposal was characterized as a step backward for ecosystem decentralization. To foster independent quality assurance, the team recommended that the proposal be split into independent parts, separating development from performance monitoring, and that node-agnostic elements be moved to neutral, community-governed spaces.
