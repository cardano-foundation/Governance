## Summary

This meeting focused on a detailed technical and financial evaluation of three high-value proposals submitted by IOG. The team reviewed the IOG and Midgard Labs L2 Scalability initiative, identifying a significant budget imbalance and a lack of clear deliverables for the Hydra component. Technical review of the Cardano High Assurance proposal highlighted the critical value of formal verification tools like Blaster, but raised concerns regarding personnel costs and redundant containerized setup environments. Finally, the team evaluated the Vacuum Labs Plutus Usability upgrades, concluding that the proposal lacks clear integration funding for downstream tool maintainers and replicates workstreams already covered by core maintenance budgets.

## Details

- **Midgard Labs L2 Scalability Initiative Evaluation:** The team reviewed the IOG and Midgard Labs scalability proposal, which requests approximately 10.5 million ada to deliver Layer 2 infrastructure. Technical advisors noted a significant structural imbalance, as the Midgard optimistic rollup represents only 9% of the requested budget despite the proposal’s dual framing. Furthermore, much of the outlined Hydra roadmap overlaps with previously funded public roadmap items, making it difficult to distinguish new work from ongoing activities. While acknowledging the general necessity of Layer 2 solutions for enterprise scaling, the team favored encouraging a resubmission that clearly defines milestones and clarifies the exact funding boundary. Further clarity may assist with solving these concerns.
- **State Channel Limitations and Technical Adoption Hurdles:** Deliberations highlighted operational frustrations with Hydra’s current state channel model, specifically the lack of dynamic membership. Under the current architecture, any configuration change or upgrade requires bringing the entire channel down and conducting manual key exchanges, creating friction for enterprise applications requiring continuous availability. Some experts argued that optimistic rollup models like Midgard present a more viable long-term architectural path for the ecosystem. However, because 90% of the proposal focuses on maintaining the existing Hydra stack rather than accelerating rollup development, the current structure fails to address immediate enterprise scaling requirements.
- **Cardano High Assurance Proposal and Financial Analysis:** The team evaluated the 13 million ada High Assurance proposal, a collaborative effort involving TXPipe, No Witness Labs, M-Labs, Lantern, Harmonic Labs, SCY, and Midgard Labs. The initiative aims to expand the Blaster formal verification tool and deliver a preconfigured developer environment. While the technical value of formal verification was widely supported as a defense against smart contract vulnerabilities, the budget raised concerns. At an estimated cost of nearly 1 million ada per developer headcount for the core team, the proposal was deemed highly inflated. A debate occurred regarding whether the public treasury should fund corporate profit markups or limit allocations strictly to non-offloadable costs, leading to a recommendation to reject the current application while inviting a more reasonably priced resubmission. Beyond the financial concerns, the team analyzed the practical adoption risks associated with the High Assurance proposal. It was observed that the developer audience for formal verification tools remains relatively narrow, as the vast majority of smart contract developers do not currently write basic property tests. While tools like Blaster represent impressive engineering, there is a risk of allocating significant treasury resources to a highly specialized utility that may remain largely unused by the broader community. Additionally, the proposed "one-click" developer environment was identified as redundant given existing options like Ignite and Yaci Devkit, which have successfully served the developer community for several years.
- **Plutus Performance and Usability Upgrades Alignment:** A technical review of the 12 million ada Vacuum Labs collaboration proposal to enhance Plutus performance was conducted. The proposal focuses on three workstreams: reducing execution costs, expanding untyped Plutus core capabilities, and streamlining compiler architecture. While certain features like built-in data casing are desired, technical advisors warned that retroactively adding built-ins to previous Plutus versions creates significant downstream maintenance burdens for independent compiler maintainers, such as Aiken, Scalus, and Pebble. The team recommended opposing the current proposal due to the absence of clear integration budgets for these downstream maintainers and the lack of defined acceptance criteria.
- **Core Protocol Upgrades and Overlapping Workstreams:** The team evaluated the organizational overlaps between the Plutus usability upgrades, the High Assurance proposal, and the core Cardano Maintenance initiative. Reviewers noted that these proposals frequently feature identical engineering teams and overlapping workstreams, effectively double-counting personnel across separate budget applications.
