## Summary

The meeting focused on a technical and fiscal review of several high-cost treasury proposals. The team aligned on a recommendation for the Blockfrost proposal, expressing concerns over commercial subsidies and lack of data transparency. Technical reservations were raised regarding the risk profile of the Pogun proposal, while a broader debate on protocol architecture concluded that complex upgrades should be directed toward Layer 2 solutions rather than the core ledger. Consequently, the team formalized the vote for the Cardano Upgrades proposal and formulated a dual-option voting strategy for the Cardano Maintenance Initiative.

## Details

- **Blockfrost Proposal Proposal:** The team conducted a technical evaluation of the 8 million ada request for Project Kaye (a next-generation indexing architecture) and an operational subsidy for a free API tier. A consensus was reached to recommend a "No" vote due to a lack of transparency regarding the entity's equity structure and relationships with other core development vendors. Technical reviewers noted that the free API tier, while framed as a public good, serves as a marketing funnel for paid commercial products, and the proposal lacks verifiable metrics to justify a public operational subsidy. Furthermore, with competing services operating successfully without relying on treasury funding, and considering the entity's history of receiving significant funding rounds via Catalyst, the team affirmed that the treasury should not be utilized to subsidize the operational overhead of private enterprise.
- **Pogun Proposal:** The team analyzed the 12.3 million ada request to implement Bitcoin DeFi components on the network, featuring a credit market, a yield application, and a trust-minimized bridge with a potential repayment structure. While the strategic value of Bitcoin integration was recognized as a vital long-term research and development objective, serious concerns were raised regarding the implementation risk and lack of operational detail surrounding capital flow management. Reviewers identified a significant default risk for lenders if the underlying collateral values experienced severe volatility, and noted that the viability of the entire mechanism hinges on an unproven yield application. Due to these structural deficiencies, the team will continue to evaluate the technical feasibility of the proposed bridge framework.
- **Cardano Upgrades Proposal:** An architectural debate took place concerning the "Cardano Upgrades" proposal, focusing on the trade-offs between adding features directly to the core ledger versus deploying them as Layer 2 solutions. Technical advisors argued that implementing complex features directly on the L1 chain increases protocol complexity, introduces bugs in third-party transaction builders, and forces the entire developer ecosystem to adapt to breaking changes. Conversely, moving sophisticated business logic to overlay layers or soft-fork structures preserves application compatibility, minimizes tooling disruption, and allows for selective developer adoption. Consequently, the team agreed an "Abstain" stance for the upgrades proposal to signal that core ledger modifications must undergo more rigorous impact and integration assessments before enactment.
- **Cardano Maintenance Proposal:** The team refined its voting recommendation for the Cardano Maintenance proposal, leaning towards a "No" or “Abstain” due to the proposal's lack of tangible deliverables and highly vague cost structures compared to other active initiatives. It was decided that the final rationale should clearly articulate concerns regarding insufficiently justified budget and reporting metrics and ensure the technical feedback is constructively integrated.
- **Cardano Vision 2026 Proposal:** The team conducted an initial review of the Vision 2026 research proposal, likely to receive a "No" vote. Technical advisors observed that the proposal is framed to address major network adoption barriers but fails to provide objective proof that its proposed solutions are technically achievable. Skepticism was further heightened by an observed lack of tangible deliverables and a history of operational inactivity from the proposing team following their previous project announcements, reinforcing the team's position that treasury allocations must be conditioned on verifiable performance metrics and continuous stakeholder reporting.
