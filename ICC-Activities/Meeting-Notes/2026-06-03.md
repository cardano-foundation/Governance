## Summary

The weekly meeting focused on 12 active governance actions, with detailed reviews conducted for 6 pending items. The team finalized recommendations regarding the TWEAG proposal and the OriLife agricultural traceability initiative, highlighting the necessity for narrower, restructured scopes. The Plutus Cost Model parameter change was designated for a supportive recommendation. Strategic analyses were conducted for 5 a.m. Earth proposal and the Open-Source Program Office (OSPO) proposal. Additionally, the team evaluated the wallet business transition proposal for Eternl.

## Details

- **TWEAG Protocol Restructuring and Resubmission Strategy:** The advisory team reviewed the current TWEAG treasury withdrawal proposal. While the team finalized its recommendation to reject the current action due to concerns over its broad scope and two-year timeline, project representatives have indicated an intent to resubmit a revised, 12-month proposal with a reduced budget. This resubmission will focus on deploying Ouroboros Peras to mainnet.
- **Agricultural Traceability and Project Viability Analysis:** A review of the agricultural traceability proposal for fruit exports from OriLife reinforced a recommendation to oppose the current grant request. The project’s business model represents a public subsidy of private business operations with no clear financial return to the treasury, whereas commercial endeavors of this nature should ideally be structured as loans. Additionally, the target of onboarding 500,000 farmers within an 18-month timeline was deemed unrealistic without an extensive, local rollout plan, and the transaction volume generated remains entirely treasury-subsidized rather than organic.
- **Plutus Cost Model and Stake Pool Operator Compensation:** The team evaluated the Plutus cost model parameter change, which is currently experiencing low voter turnout. The team agreed to support the proposal immediately and coordinate a communication campaign through public channels to encourage broader voter participation before expiration.
- **5 a.m. Earth Proposal Technical Assessment:** The team conducted a review of the 5 a.m. Earth proposal, which requests 10 million ada. Although the proposal's micro-loans, land registry, and decentralized identity goals align with the strategic narrative of sustainable development, technical advisors highlighted several structural risks. The business model's daily acquisition rate to onboard 500,000 non-technical farmers within 18 months was deemed unrealistic without an extensive local rollout plan. Additionally, because the on-chain transactions are entirely subsidized by previous grants, the project lacks organic volume, requiring an estimated 270 million transactions to break even. The team recommended structuring such commercial enterprises as interest-free loans rather than direct grants.
- **Decentralized Open-Source Program Office (OSPO) Structure:** The proposal to establish an independent Open-Source Program Office (OSPO) requesting 12 million ada over 36 months was evaluated. The team recommended rejecting the proposal due to several structural deficiencies, including a lack of interim milestone reviews for multi-year budgets, high administrative overheads representing 30% of the total request, and undefined committee representation. Furthermore, the proposal fails to coordinate with existing funded developer experience initiatives or the Intersect open-source committee, and the lack of an exchange rate cap exposes the treasury to overfunding risks if market values fluctuate.
- **Scalus and Browser-Based Development Tooling:** The team reviewed the Scalus smart contracting language proposal. The project team, in coordination with other ecosystem developers, aims to develop a browser-integrated web interface similar to successful onboarding and educational platforms in other networks. While acknowledging the value of web-integrated development tools for developer onboarding, the team will wait for finalized technical assessments from SMEs before recording its final advisory position, balancing infrastructure utility against current treasury limits.
- **Wallet Business Model Transition and Sustainability:** The transition proposal for the Eternl wallet was reviewed. To transition away from historical public grants, the wallet intends to deploy a sustainable freemium model, featuring premium subscriptions for retail users and dedicated plans for enterprise clients. Reviewers supported this transitional funding model, noting that the wallet manages a significant portion of ecosystem transaction volume (estimated between 10% and 18%) and that the repayment terms are structured realistically as a loan triggered upon reaching profitability thresholds, making it an appropriate use of treasury resources to preserve vital, independent infrastructure.
