## Meeting Recap

The weekly meeting centered on a technical and economic evaluation of the OpenZeppelin treasury withdrawal proposal, which requests approximately 12 million ada over twelve months for reference implementations, a contract library, and security audit retainers. Advisors examined the balance between brand recognition and architectural necessity, weighing the potential institutional credibility and lowered developer onboarding friction against concerns regarding technical alignment with Cardano's native UTxO model. Deliberations highlighted potential redundancies with existing ecosystem work, such as CIP-113 and previously funded developer experience initiatives, as well as the absence of specified off-chain tooling and language support, prompting plans for follow-up coordination with the proposing team and Intersect to explore broader roadmap alignment.

The team also evaluated the parameter change proposal to reduce the minimum pool cost parameter from 170 ada to 75 ada. The discussion analyzed the shifting economic incentives facing stake pool operators as block rewards decline, noting that the fixed 170 ada floor absorbs a disproportionate share of rewards on single-block pools and creates a delegator penalty. Advisors evaluated whether lowering the floor would support smaller pool viability and prepare the network for future saturation parameter adjustments, referencing historical data from the previous reduction from 340 ada to demonstrate that delegation behavior remained stable without triggering a downward race on operator margins.
